## What Dust Front RTS is

Dust Front RTS is a Windows strategy game developed by rtsDimon. The official Steam description presents it as a classic real-time strategy game expanded with grand-strategy elements, a procedurally generated world and a non-linear campaign. That combination suggests two connected decision layers: immediate control of a base and army, and wider choices about territory, economy and campaign movement.

The safest way to understand the game is to start with the features explicitly named in official material. Players build bases, mine resources, process materials and use factories to manufacture military equipment. Public descriptions also mention infantry, vehicles and aviation, as well as technology branches, garrisons and a global map. Exact unit lists, costs and technology nodes should not be assumed until they appear in a dated public build.

## The core strategic loop

The confirmed systems imply a loop of **information, extraction, conversion and deployment**. First, identify useful territory and the immediate threat. Second, establish enough extraction and processing to support continued production. Third, convert that economy into factories, defenses and military forces. Finally, use those forces to protect production or achieve the current objective.

This loop is more useful than memorizing a rigid recipe. Procedural generation and a non-linear campaign can change the value of terrain, resources and routes. A build that works on one map may fail when access, enemy pressure or mission goals change. Treat every opening as a hypothesis that must be checked against the map.

## How to approach your first sessions

Begin by learning the interface and identifying which information is actually visible in the current Demo. Record the names of resources, available structures and production categories rather than relying on older videos. Confirm which features are playable now and which are only described for the future full game.

Once you can keep a small production loop running, test one change at a time. Expand earlier, delay a factory, change the balance between economy and defense, or alter the first military unit. Single-variable tests make failures useful because they show which decision changed the outcome.

## Economy and production

Think in terms of bottlenecks. A mine is not useful if materials cannot be processed, and a factory is not useful if its input supply repeatedly stops. Storage can absorb short interruptions, but excessive stockpiling can also mean resources are not being converted into useful capability. Watch the whole chain rather than one large number.

Production queues should answer a battlefield need. Maintain enough flexible capacity to replace losses, respond to new information and avoid committing the entire economy to one unverified plan. When a queue stalls, trace the problem backward: missing input, blocked processing, insufficient storage or an earlier expansion decision.

## Army planning

Official material confirms broad categories of infantry, vehicles and aviation, but not a stable public database of every unit. Build compositions around roles instead of invented statistics. You generally need vision or information, durable frontline presence, damage appropriate to the target and enough mobility or reserve capacity to respond elsewhere.

Combined arms is a decision framework, not a fixed ratio. A force should solve the current mission and remain supportable by the economy. If a composition wins one battle but exhausts replacement capacity, it may still be strategically poor.

## Campaign and global systems

The official description discusses a global map, territory-level economy, events, traders, mercenaries and garrisons. These features are important to the game's identity, but current Demo availability must be checked separately. Do not treat a store-page feature list as proof that every element is active in the public Demo.

When those systems are available, evaluate choices by their downstream effect on production and force readiness. Territory is valuable because of what it enables, not only because it changes map color. Garrisons, routes and economic access may determine whether a tactical victory can be sustained.

## A reliable learning order

1. Confirm the current version and available modes.
2. Learn resource extraction, processing and storage.
3. Establish a repeatable base layout.
4. Test factory queues and replacement capacity.
5. Learn the roles of units visible in the current build.
6. Add campaign and global-map decisions only after confirming their implementation.

This guide will change as public evidence improves. Specific values and named systems should always be checked against the current build rather than copied from an undated source.
