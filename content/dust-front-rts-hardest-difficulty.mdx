> **Direct answer:** On the highest Dust Front RTS difficulty, prioritize information, economic continuity and recovery over a greedy opening. Exact modifiers and enemy timing need a dated Demo or full-game source.

## Confirm the difficulty rules first

The public material available for this project does not establish a verified table of highest-difficulty modifiers. Before following a difficulty guide, record the current build, mode and setting. Do not copy enemy health, resource penalties or attack timings from an unlabeled video.

The principles below are a survival framework, not a claim about hidden numerical rules.

## Protect the first chain

High pressure punishes interruptions. Complete a small extraction and processing loop before adding optional capacity. Keep enough reserve to repair, replace or adapt when the first threat appears. An economy that remains active through pressure is more valuable than an early expansion that creates a larger exposed footprint.

## Information before commitment

The exact scouting mechanics still require current-build evidence, but the principle is clear: avoid spending the entire opening army without learning where pressure is coming from. Test what the game reveals and how long that information remains useful. Treat generic RTS scouting habits as player advice until Dust Front RTS-specific evidence exists.

Use information to change the plan. A known route can justify defense; an exposed resource area can justify delay; an enemy production signal can justify a different queue.

## Build a recovery path

Harder settings turn one lost structure or force into a cascading problem. Keep replacement space, a protected core and a route for reinforcements. Avoid layouts where a single attack removes extraction, storage and production together.

The first defense should buy time for a response, not promise that the perimeter is invulnerable. If it cannot be supported or repaired, it may delay the loss without solving the underlying problem.

## Spend with a trigger

Every major early expense should answer a known need: secure an input, protect the chain, create a response force or open a mission route. If the need disappears, pause the plan and reassess. This is especially important when the current build's costs and production times are not yet stable.

Keep production queues short until the next threat is visible. A long queue can lock the economy into a response that the map no longer requires.

## Learn from failure

After a failed run, identify the first irreversible decision. Was expansion too early? Did storage or processing stall? Did the first force lack information or a retreat path? Did defense protect the wrong structure? Change one variable and test again.

Avoid calling a run “unwinnable” without checking whether the failure began before the first attack. Difficulty strategy improves fastest when the economic cause is recorded alongside the battle result.

## High-difficulty checklist

1. Record version and settings.
2. Establish a complete, readable resource chain.
3. Reserve space and resources for recovery.
4. Test information tools before committing the main force.
5. Add production for a named threat or objective.
6. Protect the structures that keep the chain running.
7. Reassess after first contact.
