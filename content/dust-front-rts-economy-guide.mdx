> **Direct answer:** Manage the Dust Front RTS economy as a connected flow from extraction through processing and storage to factory output. Diagnose the slowest stage before adding more buildings.

## Start with the production chain

Official material confirms mining, processing and industrial production as core systems. That means the economy should be evaluated as a chain rather than a single resource total. A weak link can leave expensive downstream facilities idle even when another material appears abundant.

Map the current build's actual flow. Record what is extracted, what requires processing, where it is stored and what each available production category consumes. Do not import resource names or values from an undated video.

## Find the active bottleneck

A bottleneck is the stage limiting useful output. If processors wait for raw input, improve extraction or transport before adding more processors. If processed materials accumulate while factories stop, inspect factory capacity, queue choices and other required inputs. If everything stops after a raid, the real bottleneck may be base resilience.

Check flow over time. A temporary shortage during expansion is different from a permanent structural imbalance.

## Balance stock and conversion

Some reserve is valuable because it absorbs interruptions and funds emergency changes. Too much reserve can indicate that resources are not being converted into information, defense or military capability. The correct amount depends on mission risk and replacement time, so avoid publishing an unsupported universal target.

Think about what the reserve protects. A correction reserve allows a missed structure, replacement or new production response. A random stockpile without a decision attached provides less strategic value.

## Expand when the chain is ready

Expansion increases potential output and the area that must be protected. Before claiming a new resource area, confirm that the current chain is stable, the route is defensible and the added input will serve an actual production need. Expansion that cannot be processed simply relocates the bottleneck.

Also consider recovery. If the expansion is lost, can the core base continue producing, or does the entire economy depend on one exposed location?

## Coordinate factories with income

Factory capacity should match sustainable input. Adding factories because queues feel slow can backfire when the economy cannot feed them. Begin with short queues, observe consumption and add capacity after the resource flow proves it can support more.

Different production roles may compete for the same processed input. Prioritize the force or structure that solves the immediate operational problem, then rebuild flexibility after the threat passes.

## Protect economic continuity

Defense should protect the function of the base. Mines, processors, storage, energy or connection routes may be more important than a single frontline structure. Separate critical elements enough that one attack does not disable every stage, while keeping movement and reinforcement practical.

Maintain a recovery path: replacement space, a reserve, and a route for units to reach damaged areas. This converts a raid from a game-ending event into a temporary reduction in throughput.

## Use economy data carefully

Do not publish precise output, cost or timing values without a current-build source. When values become available, attach the Demo version and review date. If two sources disagree, retain both as version evidence instead of averaging them.

Useful economy notes explain behavior as well as numbers: which stage stalled, what triggered the shortage and how long recovery took.

## Economy review checklist

- Is every extraction structure feeding a useful downstream stage?
- Is processing limited by input, capacity or interruption?
- Are factories idle because of missing materials or poor queue choices?
- Does storage protect against interruptions or hide unused surplus?
- Can the base recover if one resource area is lost?
- Is the next expansion tied to a specific production requirement?

Use this checklist after each major battle. Production problems often begin several decisions before the moment a queue finally stops.
