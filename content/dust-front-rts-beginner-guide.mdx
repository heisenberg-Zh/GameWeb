> **Direct answer:** In your first Dust Front RTS operation, identify the objective, establish a small sustainable resource chain, secure the base footprint and add production only when the economy can keep it active.

## Read the mission before building

Do not place structures immediately just because the game allows it. First identify the objective, available resources, likely approach routes and any visible time pressure. Dust Front RTS is described as combining RTS and grand-strategy systems in a procedural world, so the correct opening can depend on conditions rather than a universal sequence.

Check the current build number or date before following a guide. The game remains listed as coming soon, and Demo systems can change. If a menu, resource or structure from an older source is absent, treat the current build as authoritative.

## Establish the minimum economy

Public material confirms mining, processing and industrial production. Your first goal is therefore not maximum expansion; it is a complete working chain. Identify what is extracted, what must be processed and which input your first useful production structure consumes.

Keep enough reserve to correct an early mistake. Spending every resource on a large factory or unnecessary extension can leave no capacity for power, storage, defense or replacement production. Add each layer only after confirming the previous layer is working.

## Build a base that can recover

A beginner base should be readable. Keep related production elements close enough to understand the flow, but avoid creating one tightly packed target where a single attack disrupts everything. Leave clear reinforcement and movement routes. Protect the structures that keep the economy running, not only the most visually impressive building.

Plan for failure before it happens. Ask where a replacement structure could fit, how units reach the threatened side and whether losing one connection stops the entire production chain. A slightly less efficient layout that is easier to repair is often better for learning.

## Add production with a purpose

Factories are confirmed as a central feature, but current costs, times and exact categories need version-specific evidence. Before adding production capacity, decide what problem it solves. You may need a first defensive force, a mobile response group or a unit able to investigate the map.

Avoid filling a long queue without checking consumption. A queue that looks productive can quietly drain the inputs needed for expansion or emergency defense. Start short, watch the flow and extend the queue after confirming that the economy remains stable.

## Use broad unit roles

Official information names infantry, vehicles and aviation. It does not justify publishing an exact beginner composition without current-build verification. Use roles instead: information, frontline presence, damage, mobility and support. Build only roles that exist in the version you are playing.

Keep new units near a safe route until you understand their movement and engagement behavior. Sending the first expensive group deep into unexplored territory turns one information mistake into an economic setback.

## Scout through controlled tests

The exact reconnaissance rules still need Demo evidence, so do not assume general RTS habits are official mechanics. You can still learn by testing what reveals terrain, how long information remains useful and which movements expose the base. Record observations as player findings, not fixed rules.

When new information arrives, translate it into a decision. An exposed route may require a defensive position; enemy production may require a different unit role; an uncontested resource area may justify expansion. Scouting has value only when it changes the plan.

## Beginner mistakes to avoid

- Expanding before the first production chain is stable.
- Building factories that the economy cannot continuously supply.
- Treating a store-page feature as proof that it is active in the Demo.
- Copying an old build order without checking version and map conditions.
- Using every unit in one group and leaving no reserve.
- Defending the perimeter while ignoring mines, processing and reinforcement routes.

## A first-session checklist

Confirm the mission and version. Find the initial resource flow. Establish extraction and processing. Reserve space for production and recovery. Create a small force with a clear purpose. Test one route at a time. After the session, record what stopped production and what caused the first major loss.

That record is more valuable than a claimed perfect opening. Dust Front RTS is still evolving, and a beginner who understands why a chain failed will adapt faster than one who memorized an outdated sequence.
