> **Direct answer:** Build Dust Front RTS armies by mission role: information, frontline presence, damage, mobility and recovery. Start with the categories visible in your build instead of assuming a fixed “best” ratio.

## Why there is no universal best army

The game is described as combining procedural worlds, a non-linear campaign and infantry, vehicles and aviation. Those conditions change what the army must accomplish. A force that protects a processing site does not need the same balance as a force that crosses an exposed map.

The public materials do not provide enough stable unit data to publish a definitive composition. This page offers a decision framework and leaves exact unit names and values to future evidence.

## Five useful roles

**Information** tells you what is ahead and whether a route is safe. **Frontline presence** holds ground or buys time. **Damage** solves the immediate target problem. **Mobility** lets the force respond or exploit an opening. **Recovery** means replacements, reserves and a route back to the economy.

Not every mission needs equal investment in all five. A defensive operation may emphasize frontline presence and recovery, while an expansion operation may need information and mobility first.

## Build from the objective

Name the objective in one sentence before selecting units. If the objective is to hold a base, prioritize a force that can delay pressure while factories continue. If the objective is to secure a resource area, prioritize information, a durable escort and a retreat route. If the objective is to punish an exposed production site, preserve a reserve for the counterattack.

This process prevents the common mistake of building an impressive force that cannot reach or sustain its target.

## Economy sets the ceiling

An army is only as strong as its replacement path. Connect the composition to extraction, processing, storage and factory output. If one role consumes a scarce input, account for what must be delayed elsewhere. A smaller force with continuous reinforcement can outperform a larger one that exhausts its economy.

Keep a reserve when the map or enemy information is uncertain. The reserve does not need to be a second complete army; it can be a response element that buys time for the main force to recover.

## Reassess after contact

The first engagement is information. Observe what the enemy can reach, how the current force performs and which role is missing. Add, replace or withdraw based on that evidence. Do not double down solely because the first queue already contains a unit type.

Record the build and mission when comparing compositions. A result without context is not a reliable recommendation.

## Composition review

- Does the force have a way to learn what is ahead?
- Can it hold long enough for the objective to finish?
- Does it have the damage or role needed for the target?
- Can it move or disengage when the route changes?
- Can the economy replace its likely losses?
- Is a reserve available for the next piece of information?
